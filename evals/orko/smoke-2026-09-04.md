## 4. Ambiguities and guesses

Ordered roughly by how much they could change a run's outcome.

**A1 — Duplicate findings across seats have no dedupe rule. (`references/linear.md:40`)**
The contract says "one issue per finding, never a single issue summarizing a seat's report. A seat that raised four findings produces four issues." It never addresses the case that actually happened twice in this run: three seats independently raising the *same* finding. Read literally, the broken pytest collection would have produced three near-identical issues attributed to `requirements`, `architecture`, and `testability`; the import-block finding would have produced two.
*Guess:* one issue per distinct finding, attributed to the seat that raised it first or most usefully, naming the corroborating seats in the body. That is what I did (JRF-45, JRF-49, JRF-51, JRF-61, JRF-62, JRF-68 all carry a corroboration line). The opposite reading is defensible and would have made the issue list ~28 instead of 22.
*Why it matters:* corroboration across independent seats is orko's strongest signal, and collapsing it into one issue partly discards it while duplicating it makes the Linear board unreadable. The skill should say which.

**A2 — Nothing tells the conductor how to decide whether a `scope:` finding is actually out of boundaries. (`SKILL.md:113`, `references/build.md:143`)**
Both files describe the mechanics of an escalation thoroughly and the judgment not at all. Four of the eight seats marked a `scope:` finding. Three of them (the pytest collection failure) proposed remedies that genuinely violate the stated boundaries — a root `conftest.py`, a `pyproject.toml` — while a fourth seat found a remedy inside them (`tests/conftest.py`, which is under `tests/`). One seat marked the guard-status test `scope:` on the theory that it needed a new spec requirement, which it did not.
*Guess:* a `scope:` mark is a seat's recommendation to escalate, not a determination. The conductor re-tests it against the boundaries as the user stated them, and escalates only when *no* in-boundary remedy exists. I handled all four rather than escalating any, and said so in each issue body.
*Why it matters:* this is the single highest-stakes judgment in a build — escalating stops the run in both modes. A conductor that treated `scope:` as binding would have halted this run at step 2 over a finding with a clean in-boundary fix. A conductor that ignores `scope:` marks defeats the one gate the skill says cannot be pre-authorized. The rule belongs in the skill.

**A3 — Boundaries are stated in prose and matched against by eye.**
The user's boundary was "only `calc.py`, `tests/`, and `README.md`". Whether `tests/conftest.py` is inside it is obvious; whether a root `conftest.py` is outside it is obvious; but nothing in the skill turns the prose into a check, and `preflight` does not look at boundaries at all. The whole escalation gate rests on the conductor reading a sentence correctly.
*Guess:* directory prefixes are inclusive — `tests/` covers anything under `tests/`.

**A4 — Handling a finding that would edit the *other* artifact has no route. (`references/build.md:150-158`)**
Step 4 says the artifact you edit, re-validate, and re-post is the plan. The `tests` seat's scope finding argued its recommendation needed a companion spec requirement. Nothing in build.md says what to do when a plan-review finding implies a spec edit: there is no "re-post the spec at step 4" instruction, and doing so would leave the spec document updated after `ledger 2 complete` with no ledger line recording it.
*Guess:* I resolved it by re-reading the spec and concluding no spec change was needed — the design section already fixed the guard body and R7 already fixed the return status, so the new test pins an existing claim. That dissolved the problem rather than answering the question. Had a spec edit genuinely been required, I would have been improvising.

**A5 — "Say so in the step summary you post at close" describes an artifact that does not exist. (`references/build.md:118`, `:131`, `:133`, `:293`)**
Four separate places tell the conductor to record something "in the step summary" — dropped lenses, a seat raised to `fable`, a seat that failed twice. *Guess:* fold anything that would have gone there into the checkpoint message. Nothing needed folding here — no lens dropped, no seat raised to `fable`, no seat failed.

**A6 — The gate the model tiering section describes does not exist in a build. (`SKILL.md:111`, `:119`, `:125`; `references/build.md:131`)**
"Say so at the gate" appears three times in SKILL.md and once in build.md. The analysis engagement has a gate — step 2, Propose, where the user approves the seat list.
