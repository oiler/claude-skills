## Ambiguities and guesses

1. **Evergreen conflicts where the fix brings the doc back into compliance.** Step 7 says a conflict with an evergreen doc is a `defer`, "a stop sign, never resolved silently". The `click` findings are correct, and their fix (use `argparse`) brings the spec into line with `CLAUDE.md`, which fits `accept` ("the finding is correct and its fix improves the document"). An `accept` with a `--why` wouldn't be silent either. I guessed `defer`, reading the stop-sign rule as absolute. The cost is that the spec still contains a claim reviewers showed is false ("which the project already allows"). The skill should say which verdict applies when the document, not the evergreen doc, is the side at fault.
2. **No partial accept.** Several accepted fixes contained a clause that would have pre-decided a deferred finding: completeness/F-2's "Do not modify it", completeness/F-12's "No changes to `textkit/slug.py`", and the input-hyphen `--sep` test case from completeness/F-10. Some also carried a detail I didn't adopt, such as completeness/F-13's broken-pipe exit code of 0. I recorded these as `accept` and explained the omission in `--why`. The skill doesn't say whether a partly applied fix is `accept`, and it offers no way to accept one part of a finding and defer the rest.
3. **"Product decision" has no test.** Step 7 lists product decisions under `defer` but gives no way to tell one from a gap with one obvious answer. I sorted findings using MODELS.md's phrase "one conventional reading". On that basis I accepted blank-line handling, streaming, the Non-goals list, broken-pipe behavior, and Python 3.10+. Someone else could reasonably have deferred the Non-goals scope or the broken-pipe behavior.
4. **How deeply to verify.** Step 7 says to open the evidence each finding cites and "check the claim yourself". With 31 findings, many duplicated across lenses, I verified each distinct fact once and didn't rerun every command the reviewers cited. The skill doesn't say whether a spot-check is enough or every command must be rerun.
5. **When findings are "the same problem".** Where two findings overlapped only in part (accuracy/F-3 and completeness/F-4 on `--sep`; accuracy/F-7, a nit, and completeness/F-7, a major, on the `main` contract), I treated them as the same problem. The skill has no rule for partial overlap or for duplicates rated at different severities.
6. **Batching `decide` and the quoting rule.** "Single-quote each `--why`" assumes one literal command per call. The skill doesn't say whether batching is allowed. The script's own comment expects one `decide` per finding, often as parallel Bash calls. With 31 findings I batched, and I used double-quoted `"$VAR"` values: safe, because the values were assigned in single quotes and expansion doesn't re-evaluate backticks, but not the letter of the rule. A batched command with a shell function also wouldn't match the `allowed-tools` patterns. This session ran with permission checks bypassed, so that didn't matter here. Under normal permissions it would prompt.
7. **Step 8's Fable sentence.** "A document that turns on a novel decision belongs here" doesn't say what counts as novel, or whether "belongs here" means naming the Fable escalation explicitly. I judged the open decisions routine (a parser choice, a separator's semantics, an entry point) and left Fable out.
8. **"Then stop" and trailing text.** Step 8 says to paste the output, write Recommended, and stop. I added two sentences after it. The skill doesn't say whether anything may follow the summary.
9. **Background dispatch.** Step 4 doesn't say whether to set `run_in_background`, and step 5's "when every reviewer has returned" implies waiting for them. Both modes work. With background dispatch I got three separate completion notifications, which cost three turns.

2. **No partial-accept rule** (ambiguity 2). The fix is to allow `accept` with a `--why` that names what was left out, or to require splitting into `accept` plus `defer`.

4. **Batching and permissions are unaddressed** (ambiguity 6). The fix is to state one literal `decide` per Bash call so the `allowed-tools` patterns match, or to say batching is fine and what it costs in permission prompts.
